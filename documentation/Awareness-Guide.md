## The Threat Is Real
Every day, thousands of deceptive emails are crafted by cybercriminals to breach our defenses. They do not just target computers; they target **human psychology**. A single careless click can compromise corporate networks, expose customer data, and lead to ransomware deployment.

---

## 5 Critical Phishing Warning Signs to Watch For

```
 ⚠️ [1] LOOK-ALIKE & SPOOFED SENDERS
     Always scrutinize the actual sender address, not just the friendly display name.
     • Impostor: "Apex Trust Bank" <support@apextrust-secure-auth.xyz>
     • Legitimate: "Apex Trust Bank" <support@apextrust.com>

 ⚠️ [2] MANUFACTURED PANIC & ARTIFICIAL URGENCY
     Threats of account suspension within 12–24 hours, legal lawsuits, or missed delivery
     penalties are designed to make you act impulsively without thinking.

 ⚠️ [3] SUSPICIOUS HYPERLINKS & MISMATCHED DESTINATIONS
     Hover your mouse cursor over any link before clicking.
     Does the destination URL match the company's official domain?
     Beware of unusual domains (.xyz, .info, .buzz, .top) and shortened URLs.

 ⚠️ [4] DEMANDS FOR PASSWORDS, OTPs, OR PAYMENT DETAILS
     Legitimate banks, logistics providers, and IT administrators will NEVER ask you to
     email or enter your password, MFA code, or PIN via an unverified web link.

 ⚠️ [5] GENERIC SALUTATIONS & UNEXPECTED ATTACHMENTS
     Beware of emails opening with "Dear Customer", "Attention Employee", or "Valued Member",
     especially those carrying unexpected attachments (.zip, .html, .iso, or macro docs).
```

## Essential Phishing Prevention Best Practices

| ✅ DO | ❌ NEVER |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Verify Out-of-Band**: Independently contact the organization using known official contact numbers or bookmarks. | **Never click direct links** in unsolicited emails requesting account verification or credential entry. |
| **Inspect Email Headers**: Check the sender's full email address and Return-Path domain before replying. | **Never open unexpected attachments** such as `.zip`, `.html`, or `.docm` files from unknown senders. |
| **Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**: Always enable MFA on all internal and external accounts. | **Never approve unexpected MFA push notifications** or share One-Time Passwords (OTPs) with anyone. |
| **Bookmark Critical Portals**: Navigate to banking, payroll, and cloud services using saved, verified bookmarks. | **Never wire funds or settle invoices** without secondary voice verification via verified phone numbers. |
| **Report Suspicious Messages**: Forward potential threats immediately to the Security Operations Center (SOC). | **Never assume an email is safe** simply because it contains official-looking company logos. |

## What To Do: The 5-Step Response Procedure

```
    [1] STOP    ───>  Do NOT click links, download attachments, or reply.
          │
    [2] VERIFY  ───>  Contact the sender independently via verified official channels.
          │
    [3] REPORT  ───>  Click the "Report Phishing" ribbon button or notify SOC immediately.
          │
    [4] DELETE  ───>  Purge the malicious email from your Inbox and Trash folder.
          │
    [5] WARN    ───>  Inform colleagues or teammates who might be targeted by the same lure.
```
